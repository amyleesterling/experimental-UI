Applied, a test page

The Pyr profile, full screen

A proposal for the player profile in Pyr, put here to be looked at before it goes into the game. Three changes: the profile fills the window, the left column gives cells completed the same today, past 7 days and all time windows that edits already have, and each of the two gets a small curve of the running total since the player's first day, drawn once when the profile opens. Second round, after Ames tried it in the game: room at the edges, cells before edits to match the middle column, the Days row as three plain columns, the second Cells block renamed Annotations, and the middle column kept at its old width so the achievement tiles stay close together.

What is real here: the stylesheet is the game's own, carried across unchanged, so the columns, tabs, tiles and achievement grid are the live ones. The totals are Ames's own from her profile on 11 October 2026. What is not: the day by day record behind the curves is generated to add up to those totals and is not her history, and the names on the achievements are the art files' names. Nothing here signs in or sends anything. Visual component only.

Full screen

Open the profile

The frame takes the whole window instead of a 1280 pixel box in the middle of it, with room at its edges. The left and right columns grow with the window, the middle one keeps its width, each scrolls on its own, and the orbiting edge light runs round the edge of the screen. Escape or the cross closes it. The numbers roll up and the curves draw once, on open, as they would in the game.

Best looked at in a full size window. Phones already open the profile full screen.

The left column

Edits and cells, three windows each, and the curve under them

Today, past 7 days and all time come from the same rows the curve is drawn from, counted once: an edit log row per merge or cut, a completion row per cell with the moment it was completed. The shaded band at the right end of each curve is the past 7 days, the brighter sliver is today, so the three numbers are also three places on the line. The curve draws from the first day to today in 1.4 seconds, the running total rides the pen, and one ring marks the arrival. Hover or touch the curve to read any day. Under reduced motion the curve lands finished.

For the game

What would change in Pyr

  • The profile is full screen on desktop, with 40 pixels of room at the sides. The shell fills the window on every tab; the Datasets, Settings and Admin Hub bodies keep their widths, centred. The middle column keeps its width so the achievement tiles stay close; the left and right columns take the room.
  • Cells first, then edits in the left column, the order of the middle column.
  • Cells completed gets today, past 7 days and all time, per dataset like edits, read from the completions list with each cell's completion time.
  • A curve of the running total under each, from the player's first day on that dataset to today, drawn once on open, with the past 7 days and today marked on it.
  • The old Cells block becomes Annotations: its Completed, Identified and Total tiles go (the all time window above is the completed count from a better source), the annotations number stays. The Days row is three plain columns, no boxes.
  • The recent cells list keeps the browser's own history; when that is empty (a new device, a test build on another address) it lists the cells completed on this dataset from the server, the same rows as the numbers.
  • Completions are teal on the 14 day chart, matching the Cell Completions track and the cells pill, instead of amber.
  • Two words fixed on the way: "Edits in EyeWire II" becomes "Edits in Pyr", and "Favorite Badge" becomes "Favorite Achievement".
