Applied, a test page

The Pyr profile, full screen

A proposal for the player profile in Pyr, put here to be looked at before it goes into the game. Three changes: the profile fills the window, the left column gives cells completed the same today, past 7 days and all time windows that edits already have, and each of the two gets a small curve of the running total since the player's first day, drawn once when the profile opens. Second round, after Ames tried it in the game: room at the edges, cells before edits to match the middle column, the Days row as three plain columns, the second Cells block renamed Annotations, and the middle column kept at its old width so the achievement tiles stay close together.

What is real here: the stylesheet is the game's own, carried across unchanged, so the columns, tabs, tiles and achievement grid are the live ones. The totals are Ames's own from her profile on 11 October 2026. What is not: the day by day record behind the curves is generated to add up to those totals and is not her history, and the names on the achievements are the art files' names. The range under each curve (all time, 90, 30 or 7 days) is one shared choice kept in this browser, and the cells identified count beside the name is a demo number. Nothing here signs in or sends anything. Visual component only.

Full screen

Open the profile

The frame takes the whole window instead of a 1280 pixel box in the middle of it, with room at its edges. Third round, Ames's idea: the left and middle columns become one area of rows, one per track. Each row is the latest Achievement won, the stats and curve for the same thing, and the other Achievements won on that track, on one line. The cell completion numbers are made up so the row reads as it would for a player with 118 cells, and the recently completed cells list is a placeholder. The right column is unchanged. Escape or the cross closes it. The numbers roll up and the curves draw once, on open, as they would in the game.

Best looked at in a full size window. Phones already open the profile full screen.

The left column

Cells and edits, the curve, then three windows each

Today, past 7 days and all time come from the same rows the curve is drawn from, counted once: an edit log row per merge or cut, a completion row per cell with the moment it was completed. The shaded band at the right end of each curve is the past 7 days, the brighter sliver is today, so the three numbers are also three places on the line. The curve sits above its numbers and draws from the first day to today in 1.4 seconds, the running total rides the pen, and one ring marks the arrival. Hover or touch the curve to read any day. Under reduced motion the curve lands finished.

For the game

What would change in Pyr

  • The profile is full screen on desktop, with 40 pixels of room at the sides. The shell fills the window on every tab; the Datasets, Settings and Admin Hub bodies keep their widths, centred. The middle column keeps its width so the achievement tiles stay close; the left and right columns take the room.
  • One row per track across the old left and middle columns: the latest Achievement at the left, the stats and curve beside it, the other Achievements won on the same line. Cells, then edits, then days. The username is the title. Each row has one head: its label, a circuit trace that draws out to a pad, a second pad the same small distance on in every row, and from it the bar toward the next Achievement, lit as far as the count has come, with a quiet note of how many to go at its end. All time stands out in the display face the career totals use, in the track's colour. The profile counts work in Pyr on all of its datasets. On a laptop screen the rows draw smaller so the list under them is in reach.
  • Cells completed gets today, past 7 days and all time, per dataset like edits, read from the completions list with each cell's completion time.
  • A curve of the running total above each, from the player's first day on that dataset to today, drawn once on open, with the past 7 days and today marked on it.
  • No scroll. The three rows take their natural height and the last row takes what is left, with the recently completed cells scrolling in their own box. Special Awards and the count of cells identified sit beside the name; the Scout report, and for the builder Building Pyr under it, have the last row's right half.
  • Days helping science in Pyr shows the latest Days Achievement beside the total; streak and best streak stay on the right under Streak. Annotations are a column of the Scout report, not a row of their own. Achievement names are tooltips, not labels, and the featured one is left out of its row.
  • The recent cells list keeps the browser's own history; when that is empty (a new device, a test build on another address) it lists the cells completed on this dataset from the server, the same rows as the numbers.
  • One colour key. Cells are sky blue, edits gold and days purple, the colours of their Achievement sections, on the labels, the curves and the Last 14 Days chart. The View all tile is a round button with a drawn arrow, and the shelf line under the tiles is gone.
  • Two words fixed on the way: "Edits in EyeWire II" becomes "Edits in Pyr", and "Favorite Badge" becomes "Favorite Achievement".
